# A short usability session

This is a plan, not completed research. No participant findings are claimed.

## Who to ask

Start with 3 to 5 people who review requests or coordinate work. Someone with operations or team management experience is more useful here than someone judging only the colors. Include a phone user and, where possible, someone who uses keyboard or assistive technology. This small round can reveal problems; it cannot prove that the design works for everyone.

Allow about 20 minutes. Ask permission before taking notes or recording. Use participant codes, not names, in public notes. Keep real customer or mission information out of the session.

## Opening

“I'm working on a dashboard concept. Everything in it is fictional. I'm testing the design, not you. Please say what you're looking for and what you expect to happen. If something is confusing, that is useful for me to hear.”

Do not explain the controls first. Start from the default view for each task. Ask “What would you try next?” before helping. Record any help you give.

## Tasks

1. **Plan a follow up.** “You are getting ready for a team check in. Find a request that is late, tell me who owns it, and explain the next step.” Observe whether the overview, status, and details make sense. Several answers are valid. OP-101 belongs to Alex Morgan; it is waiting on information and needs the missing checklist confirmed and a new date agreed.
2. **Look ahead.** “What does the People team have due within the next 30 days?” The intended view has one open request. Ask whether the participant expects overdue work to appear here. Do not lead them toward the helper text.
3. **Read the numbers.** “What does 75% completed on time mean here? What can this screen tell you about team capacity?” In the default view it means 3 of 4 completed requests were on time. Counts do not establish capacity or effort. Ask what the partial current week means before comparing completion bars.
4. **Recover from an empty result.** “Search for a request called Travel policy, then get back to all requests.” No match is expected. Watch how they recover and whether the disabled export makes sense.
5. **Take the view with you.** “Show only overdue requests and export that list.” The file should contain 3 fictional rows, with work status and schedule as separate fields. Verify the downloaded file manually. The application has no sharing or notification workflow.
6. **Try a phone.** Repeat the first task on a phone. Ask what feels harder and what information, if any, is missing.

## After the sessions

Group repeated problems. Fix anything that blocks completion or causes a wrong interpretation first. Keep a before image, the observation that prompted the change, the change itself, and the result of another check. Publish anonymized findings with the participant count and limitations. Do not turn assumptions into quotes or invent a persona from this plan.

## Interview walkthrough

Before promoting the project, explain it aloud without reading the case study: who it is for, the main task, two choices and their tradeoffs, what you contributed, and what you would change next. Make one small change yourself and explain how you checked it. That makes the project easier to discuss honestly in an interview.
